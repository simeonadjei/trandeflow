Here's exactly how it happened — and why you should be skeptical of it:

How the algorithm works:

Downloads ~3,000 1h candles (BTC + ETH combined, ~90 days)
Groups them into 168 buckets: every (weekday × hour) combination — Monday 00:00, Monday 01:00 … Sunday 23:00
For each bucket, counts: how many candles closed higher than they opened?
Sunday 14:00 UTC had 18 candles across both assets, and all 18 happened to be green → 100%
Why 100% is misleading: 90 days ÷ 7 days = ~13 Sundays. Multiply by 2 assets = ~26 samples at most for any given weekday+hour slot. Sunday 14:00 only got 18, meaning some Sundays were missing from the data. With 18 coin flips, landing 18 heads is unlikely but not impossible — and across 168 slots you're essentially running 168 different coin flip experiments and picking the most extreme result. That's pure overfitting.

The real consequence: The next Sunday 14:00 UTC is 4.5 days away. The bot will sit completely idle until then — and when it finally trades, the "100% win rate" backing it is based on 18 data points, not 180.

The fix: drop weekday granularity entirely and use the 24-hour buckets only. Each hour slot then gets ~180 samples (3,000 ÷ 24 × 2 assets) — far more reliable. The best hour across the full dataset is a much sturdier signal than the best weekday+hour, and the bot can trade every day instead of once a week.
